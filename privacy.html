Keeping Tabs privacy policy

Version 1.0 · effective 7 September 2026

Keeping Tabs is a food diary. It is made by Pyrokinesis Ltd, a company registered in New Zealand (NZBN 9429048827553), at 29 Karamu Street, Ngaio, Wellington 6035, New Zealand. Pyrokinesis Ltd is the agency responsible for the personal information described here under the Privacy Act 2020, and the data controller for it under UK and EU data protection law, which reach us because the app is offered to people in those places.

The short version.

Your food diary is stored on your phone and is never uploaded. We cannot read it. What leaves your device is the name and email address your sign-in provider gives us, so that one account can reach your other devices — and, only if you say yes, your address for occasional emails about the app.

There is no advertising, no analytics, no tracking, and no third party is paid for anything you do here.

1 What stays on your phone

Everything you record. Every food you log, the amounts, the times, your meals and recipes, your targets and programme, your weigh-ins, your profile, and any photograph you take of a nutrition label. All of it is written to a database on the device and to a container shared with the Keeping Tabs widgets and Apple Watch app on the same device.

None of it is sent to us. There is no server holding your diary, so there is nothing for us to read, sell, lose or be compelled to hand over. If you delete the app, that data is deleted with it.

You can take a copy at any time: the app exports your diary as CSV or JSON, and the file goes wherever you send it.

Your account

You sign in with Apple, with Google, or with an email address and password. Your provider gives us a stable identifier and, where you permit it, your name and email address. Sign in with Apple can give a private relay address instead of your real one, and that works exactly as well here.

Your name is used to fill in your profile so you do not have to type it, and to draw the initial on your avatar. Your email address identifies your account and, where a password is used, is how you sign in.

The mailing list

You are asked once whether you would like occasional email about the app. The answer is no unless you say otherwise. Whichever way you answer, we record: your account identifier, your email address, your answer, the date, the exact wording you were shown and its version, where in the app you were asked, your locale region, and the app version. We keep the wording because an agreement to something you cannot see afterwards is not much of an agreement, and because we may have to show that consent was properly obtained.

You can change your answer at any time in the app, and every marketing email carries an unsubscribe link.

Barcode lookups

The app ships with a catalogue of food on the device, so most barcodes are answered without any connection at all. When a scan finds nothing there, the barcode — and only the barcode — is sent to Open Food Facts, an open database run by a French non-profit association. The request carries no account, no device identifier and no cookies, and the app refuses cookies in both directions. Open Food Facts will hold this in its server logs under its own policy.

This can be switched off in the app under You → Scanning. With it off, the app asks nothing of anybody.

3 What we never collect

  • Your food diary, your weight, your targets, or anything else you record.
  • Your location, at any precision.
  • Your contacts, photos, calendar or files. The app can read a photograph of a nutrition label, which it processes on the device and does not upload.
  • Advertising identifiers. There is no advertising in this app and no advertising SDK in it.
  • Analytics of any kind. There is no analytics or crash-reporting SDK in the app, so we do not know which screens you open or whether it crashed.

We do not track you across other apps or websites, and we do not share, sell or rent anything to anyone for their own purposes.

4 Apple Health

If you allow it, the app reads measurements from Apple Health: weight, height, date of birth, biological sex, lean body mass, resting energy, active energy and menstrual cycle. Each is switched on separately and only the ones you switch on are read.

Health data is read on the device, used there to work out your energy estimate and your targets, and never transmitted anywhere — not to us, not to Google, not to anyone. Nothing is written back to Health unless you explicitly ask the app to add a day's food to it.

5 Camera and photographs

The camera is used to scan barcodes and to photograph nutrition labels. Barcode scanning happens live on the device and no image is stored. A label photograph is read on the device using Apple's on-device text recognition, and the photograph and the text stay on the device. Neither is uploaded.

6 Payment

Keeping Tabs Complete is a one-off in-app purchase. Apple handles the transaction; we never see your card, your billing address or your name from it. The app asks Apple whether this Apple ID has bought the product and stores only the answer, on the device.

7 Who processes data for us

  • Google (Firebase Authentication and Firestore) — holds your account record and your mailing-list answer, as our processor.
  • Apple — operates Sign in with Apple and the App Store, as an independent controller under its own privacy policy.
  • Open Food Facts — receives barcode lookups as described in section 2, as an independent controller under its own policy.
  • No third-party email service is used at present: the mailing list is held in Firestore and nothing has been sent from it. If that changes, this policy will name the provider before the first message goes out.

8 Legal bases, and how long we keep things

Under UK and EU GDPR, which apply to people using the app in those places, we rely on:

  • Contract — your account record. We cannot give you an account that works across devices without one.
  • Consent — the mailing list, the barcode lookup setting, and Apple Health access. Each is asked for separately and each can be withdrawn without affecting the rest.
  • Legitimate interests — keeping a record of your mailing-list answer, including a refusal, so that we can honour it and demonstrate it.

Your account record is kept for as long as you have an account. Your mailing-list record is kept while you have an account and for seven years after it is deleted, so that an unsubscribe cannot be lost. Anything on your phone is kept until you delete it or delete the app.

9 Where data is held

Firebase stores this data in Sydney, Australia (australia-southeast1), chosen when the database was created and fixed permanently at that point.

For people in New Zealand, Principle 12 of the Privacy Act 2020 permits disclosure to a foreign person or entity where that entity is subject to privacy safeguards comparable to the Act; Australia's Privacy Act 1988 provides them, and Google's terms bind it further.

For people in the United Kingdom or the European Economic Area, this is a transfer to a country without an adequacy decision, and it is covered by Google’s Cloud Data Processing Addendum, which incorporates the European Commission’s Standard Contractual Clauses and the UK Addendum to them.

10 Children

Keeping Tabs is not directed at children and we do not knowingly collect personal data from anyone under 16. If you believe a child has given us data, write to us and we will delete it.

11 Deleting your account

You can delete your account and everything held against it from inside the app, under You → Account. Deleting it removes your account record and your mailing-list record from our systems.

Signing out is separate and lighter: it clears the account from the device without deleting anything held for it. Deleting the app removes everything stored on the device, including your diary.

12 Your rights

Wherever you are, you may ask us for a copy of the personal data we hold about you, ask us to correct it, or ask us to delete it. Your diary is on your phone and the app can export it at any time without asking us.

New Zealand. You have rights of access and correction under the Privacy Act 2020, and may complain to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner at privacy.org.nz.

United Kingdom and European Economic Area. You have rights of access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability and objection, and the right to withdraw consent at any time. You may complain to the UK Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk, or to your national supervisory authority in the EEA.

Australia. You have rights of access and correction under the Australian Privacy Principles, and may complain to the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner at oaic.gov.au.

United States. Residents of California and other states with comparable laws may request access to, or deletion of, personal information, and may not be discriminated against for asking. We do not sell or share personal information as those laws define it.

If this policy changes in a way that affects you, the app will say so before the change takes effect.
